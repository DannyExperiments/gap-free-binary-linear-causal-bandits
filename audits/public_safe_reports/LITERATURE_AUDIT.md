# Q441 literature and target check

**27 November 2026. High confidence in the source and model comparison, moderate confidence in the bounded priority check.** The candidate gives the requested polynomial-in-node-count, square-root-in-time statistical regret bound for fully observed binary linear DAGs. Its enumeration takes exponential time. It does not settle an efficient implementation with only the pair oracle from the original paper. No earlier result with the full main scope was found in the checked literature. The atomic-menu result is a credited adaptation of prior methods.

## Target and source

- Shi Feng, Nuoya Xiong and Wei Chen, *Combinatorial Causal Bandits without Graph Skeleton*, PMLR 260 (2025), 271–286. Section 3, pp. 274–275, gives binary nodes, target Y, an intercept fixed at one, nonnegative binary-linear mechanism weights, centered structural noise, and observations of all modeled nodes in the main Markovian setting. The menu includes null and atomic interventions, and can contain other hard interventions. Theorem 9 on p. 283 gives O(n³ T^(2/3) log T) without a weight gap. The paragraph that follows asks for O(√T log T) in the horizon without a weight gap. Its separate hidden-variable remark concerns a restricted extension of that older algorithm. [Proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v260/feng25a.html) · [Official PDF, p. 283](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v260/main/assets/feng25a/feng25a.pdf#page=13)
- The pinned Q441 statement expressly requires full observation. It allows joint action/parameter optimization over row confidence ellipsoids as a supplied oracle. The proof meets the statistical question and uses full observations as in that statement, but takes a different route with a finite set defined by squared predictive loss. Runtime polynomial outside the supplied oracle would be a stronger requirement. The centered exogenous mechanism yields the interventional mean recurrence; the algorithm uses the full bounded vector and does not assume coordinate independence. [Pinned Q441](https://github.com/10m-u/open-problems-catalog/blob/ca8d4d44ba3db527f19f28928c240168f545ecfe/problems/statistics_data_science/experimental-design-causal-inference.md#q441)

## Nearby primary results

- Feng and Chen’s *Combinatorial Causal Bandits* (AAAI 2023) gives regret bounds for binary generalized linear and binary linear models with relevant causal structure supplied, and a separate reduction for certain hidden-node configurations. Li et al.’s *Online Influence Maximization under Linear Threshold Model* (NeurIPS 2020) gets a square-root horizon rate in the linear-threshold diffusion setting with the network supplied and activation-time feedback. These are prior uses of linear structure and causal propagation, with different information or benchmark assumptions. [Feng–Chen](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/25917) · [Li et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/0d352b4d3a317e3eae221199fdb49651-Abstract.html)
- Yan and Tajer, *Linear Causal Bandits: Unknown Graph and Soft Interventions* (NeurIPS 2024), prove square-root-in-time regret in a linear structural-equation setting with dependence exponential in maximum directed-path length. That bound is insufficient for arbitrary depth with polynomial dependence on node count. Shahverdikondori, Etesami and Kiyavash, *Graph Learning is Suboptimal in Causal Bandits* (AISTATS 2026), bypass graph learning for general reward mechanisms. Their Theorems 4.4 and 5.3 retain combinatorial factors involving the intervention budget and the number of reward parents, which can be exponential. Their general lower bounds do not imply a lower bound on the restricted binary-linear class. [Yan–Tajer](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/2aba6ec20299931d46ddeefd5ddcb442-Abstract-Conference.html) · [Shahverdikondori et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v300/shahverdikondori26a.html)
- Russo and Van Roy’s optimistic exploration work already uses squared-loss confidence over model classes. Foster et al.’s decision-estimation theory gives general decision-making reductions whose rates depend on class-specific estimation and exploration complexity. Ordinary linear bandits assume known action features. These tools supply relevant precedents. Neither the entropy of the finite coefficient grid alone nor general finite-arm regret gives a polynomial bound for a menu of up to 3^m hard interventions. The main argument supplies a class-specific bound from the interventional mean residuals and a rowwise determinant potential. [Russo–Van Roy](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2013/hash/41bfd20a38bb1b0bec75acf0845530a7-Abstract.html) · [Foster et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.13487)
- The atomic comparison is deliberately credited. Finnian Lattimore, Tor Lattimore and Mark D. Reid’s *Causal Bandits* (NeurIPS 2016), Theorem 2 and the arXiv proof in §8, use independent rare binary roots, an affine reward bump on a single root, and the same full-feedback KL chain-rule bound for simple regret. A uniformly random played arm converts expected cumulative regret divided by T to simple regret. The companion adapts their lower-bound family using a positive common baseline so every alternative has the same positive support, with a direct cumulative proof for all m ≥ 2. The upper bound is Audibert and Bubeck’s MOSS, Theorem 5, plus the trivial T cap. The comparison is specific to the null-plus-atomic menu, and the one-root case is zero by monotonicity. [Lattimore–Lattimore–Reid, §8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.03203#page=11) · [Audibert–Bubeck, Theorem 5](https://www.learningtheory.org/colt2009/papers/022.pdf#page=3)

The search included the official source, current closely related unknown-graph works, general model-based confidence methods, influence maximization, and the atomic hard-family literature. The paper asks a statistical question; the release should make its computational limits explicit. The horizon exponent is necessary over the allowed class of menus, but optimal node-count dependence for arbitrary combinatorial menus remains open.
